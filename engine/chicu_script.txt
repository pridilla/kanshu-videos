Jealous in Chinese is 吃醋 (chīcù). It literally means eating vinegar. But why? Look at 吃 (chī). On the left is 口 (kǒu), a mouth. On the right is 乞 (qǐ), begging for food. Together, it means swallowing food! Now look at 醋 (cù). On the left is 酉 (yǒu), a wine jar. On the right is 昔 (xī), the past. Wine left in a jar for ages ferments into sour vinegar! When an Emperor tested a loyal wife with a cup of poison or sharing her husband, she drank the whole cup instantly—which was actually vinegar! That's why 吃醋 (chīcù) means the sour sting of jealousy!
